Sharia-court deeds of Algiers · 18th–19th century

Every line read by Gemini. Every page approved by an expert.

Dīwān al-Wathāʼiq turns handwritten court deeds in Maghrebi script into searchable text. Gemini makes the first reading, a second model checks it, and an expert corrects and approves each page before it is saved.

How it works

From scan to approved text, in five steps

One page at a time. Machines do the first reading and the first check; nothing is saved until an expert approves it.

  1. 01n8n

    Pick up

    A new scan is taken from the Inbox and set aside, so it is never read twice.

  2. 02Library

    Examples

    Pages already approved are shown to Gemini as worked examples of the team's conventions.

  3. 03Gemini

    Read and check

    Gemini transcribes the page; a second model compares it with the scan, line by line.

  4. 04Expert

    Review

    The expert reads the scan beside the text, corrects it, and decides.

  5. 05Drive

    Save

    The approved text is saved as a .txt beside its scan, ready to teach the next pages.

When the checker doubts a line, Gemini reads the page once more with the checker's notes. The better version is kept.

The expert can send a page back with a note. Gemini reads it again, guided by the note, and the page returns for review.

Three rules the system keeps

Nothing is saved without an expert

Gemini proposes, a second model questions, a person decides. A rejected page stays out of the archive.

The deed's own spelling stays

One output line per manuscript line, no corrected grammar, no added vowels: a diplomatic transcription.

Every approved page teaches the next

An approved scan and its text can join the library of examples, so Gemini learns the conventions of this fonds.

The archive

Fonds 1 MiOM 31 of the Archives nationales d'outre‑mer

Court deeds, waqf and notarial records from Algiers, written in Maghrebi script in the 18th and 19th centuries and kept on microfilm in Aix-en-Provence. Their handwriting, seals and abbreviations make ordinary text recognition fail.

Maghrebi letter forms, as Gemini is told to read them. The transcription writes them as ف and ق.

For experts

Review a page wherever you are

Each page reaches the expert with Gemini's text, the checker's notes and a link to the scan. The expert corrects it and makes one of three decisions.

  • Line against line. The text keeps one line per manuscript line, numbered like the scan.
  • Doubts in plain sight. Lines the checker questioned are marked, with its note in Arabic.
  • Three clear decisions. Approve, send back to Gemini with a note, or reject.

Today the review happens in a form sent by email. Next, a review desk will show the scan beside the text, one line at a time on a phone, as in the preview.
